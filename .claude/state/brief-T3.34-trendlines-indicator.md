## What Everton means (the picture he sent: InfoMoney/Binance/Toro "linhas de tendência", "padrões gráficos": trend lines, channels, wedges, triangles, flags, head & shoulders, double top/bottom)
Classical technical analysis drawn by hand: a line through 2+ swing lows (support, uptrend) or 2+ swing highs (resistance, downtrend), validated by a 3rd touch; price "respects" it (bounces) until it "breaks" it, often with a retest. Everything else in the picture (wedge, triangle, channel, flag) is two such lines with a relation (converging / parallel / diverging). This task delivers the primitive — pivots and lines — done deterministically so a strategy and the chart can use it. Patterns are T3.35 (later), on top of this.

## Deliver
1. **`hunter_indicators/patterns/pivots.py`**: swing highs/lows from OHLC bars with a confirmation window `k` (a high is a pivot if it is the max of `k` bars each side) **and** an ATR-scaled significance filter (`min_swing_atr`, default 1.0) so noise is not a pivot; pivots are only known `k` bars later (no look-ahead: expose `confirmed_at` index). Decimal in, Decimal out for prices; indexes/ints for time.
2. **`hunter_indicators/patterns/trendlines.py`**: candidate lines through pairs of same-side pivots (resistance through highs, support through lows) with slope; a line is **valid** when ≥ `min_touches` (default 3) pivots lie within `tolerance_atr` (default 0.25) of it and no close crosses it by more than `tolerance_atr` between the first and last touch ("respected"); score = touches × span − violations; keep the best non-redundant lines (angle bucket, dedupe). Output: `TrendLine(kind=support|resistance, anchors=[(idx, price)…], slope_per_bar, touches, first_idx, last_idx, valid_from_idx (= confirmation of the 3rd touch), projected(idx))`. Channel = a support and a resistance with |slope difference| small (`parallel_tol`) — output `Channel(upper, lower, width_atr)`.
3. **Events** `hunter_indicators/patterns/events.py`: `breakout` (close beyond the line by > `break_atr` (default 0.5) with RVOL ≥ `rvol_min` if provided), `retest` (within `tolerance_atr` of the broken line within `retest_bars` after a breakout, then close back in the breakout direction), `bounce` (touch within tolerance then close away ≥ `bounce_atr`). All evaluated at bar close, no look-ahead; each event carries the line id and the bar index.
4. **Tests** (`packages/indicators/tests/patterns/`): synthetic series where you know the answer — a clean uptrend with 3 lows on a line → exactly one support line with 3 touches and the right slope; a line with a 2-bar violation → invalid; parallel lines → channel; breakout with/without RVOL; retest within/after window; look-ahead test: results at bar i must not change when bars > i are appended (property test with hypothesis over random walks). Decimal precision test (no float in prices).
5. **Pictures on real data** (this is how Everton learns whether we "know how to draw"): export from the VPS ETHUSDT and SOLUSDT 15 m and 1 h candles for the last 14 days (read-only SQL → CSV → `scp`), run the detector, plot with matplotlib (dev dependency only; PNG to `.claude/state/design/trendlines/<market>-<tf>.png`): candles, pivots as dots, valid lines with their touches numbered, breakouts/retests marked. Six pictures minimum. Look at them yourself and say honestly what a human would have drawn differently (that goes into the KB page as the known limits).
6. **KB draft** `.claude/state/exp-drafts/KB-00xx-linhas-de-tendencia.md`: the rules in Portuguese for a human (o que é pivô, quando uma linha vale, o que é rompimento, reteste, canal), the parameters and their defaults, the pictures embedded (relative paths Sexta-feira will move to `obsidian/attachments/`), the limits found in 5.
